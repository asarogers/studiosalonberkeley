First-time salon visits can be nerve-wracking, especially for anyone who's had a bad experience before — a stylist who didn't understand their hair, a consultation that got rushed, a cut or color that didn't match what was asked for. I take new-client visits at Studio Salon seriously for exactly that reason. Here's what to expect, start to finish, so you walk in ready and walk out with hair you love.

## Booking: Call or Text for Now

Right now, the easiest way to book with me at Studio Salon is to call or text (510) 690-5274. Online booking is on the way — we're finishing the setup — but for new clients, a short phone or text conversation actually works better anyway. It lets me ask a few questions before we lock in a time: what service you're looking for, the current state of your hair, how much length we're working with, any color history, and whether you've worn the style before.

A knotless install and a single-process color, for example, need very different time blocks, and I'd rather build your appointment accurately than squeeze you in and rush the work. If you text, please include your name, the service you're interested in, and a couple of days that work for you. I'll text back within a business day with available windows and a deposit link if one is needed.

Don't worry about sounding formal. This is a salon, not a doctor's office. A short "Hey, I'm trying to book a retwist, here's what my hair looks like" is plenty to start the conversation.

## The Consultation: Short, Honest, No Pressure

Every first visit starts with a quick consultation in the chair before any hair gets touched. Depending on the service, this is anywhere from five to fifteen minutes. I'll ask about your hair history — past chemical processes, last haircut, how your hair usually behaves after washing — and I'll actually look at your scalp and ends.

If you're coming in for color, I'll do a strand test if there's any uncertainty. If you're coming in for locs, I'll walk you through what your starter method looks like, how long initial locking typically takes, and what to expect in the awkward months in between.

If what you asked for isn't going to work on your hair — and sometimes that's the honest answer — we'll talk through alternatives before we start, not after. I'd rather reschedule you for the right service than send you out with something you don't love. No pressure, no upsell, no guilt.

## What to Bring Based on Your Service

What you bring depends on what you booked. Here's a simple guide by service type.

### For loc appointments

Come with clean, fully dried hair unless we've agreed on a wash service that day. Bring any products you currently use on your locs so I can see what's on the hair and decide whether we need to clarify. If you've had work done elsewhere recently, a couple of photos of the previous retwist really help me see where we're starting.

### For braids and protective styles

Come with freshly washed, detangled, and blow-dried hair, or add a wash-and-blow service to your booking so we can do it together. Bring the synthetic or human hair for your install; I'll tell you exactly how many packs when we book. Bring reference photos on your phone — three or four is better than one, and screenshots of the back of the style are underrated.

### For color

Come with hair that hasn't been washed that morning. A little natural oil on the scalp actually protects it during processing. Bring photos of the color you want and, if you have them, photos of colors you don't want. If you've had any box color in the last year, tell me even if you think I won't notice.

## Getting to 2902 Sacramento St

Studio Salon is at 2902 Sacramento St in Berkeley, in the Sacramento Street corridor between Ashby and Russell. Street parking is free and usually available within a block or two, though it gets tighter in the late afternoon. If you have a longer appointment, I'd plan to arrive ten minutes early so parking doesn't stress you out.

If you're coming from further in the East Bay or from San Francisco, Ashby BART is about a fifteen-minute walk or a very short rideshare from the shop. Several AC Transit lines run along Sacramento Street too. If you're driving from Oakland, Emeryville, Richmond, or Albany, you're looking at fifteen to twenty-five minutes on a normal day, longer if you hit rush hour on the 580 or 80.

On arrival, look for the Studio Salon sign. If the door is locked — it sometimes is between appointments — text the salon line and I'll let you in. There's no big waiting room; this is an intimate studio, not a chain. Please don't arrive more than five to ten minutes early, so the client ahead of you gets their full time.

## What the Session Actually Looks Like

Once you're settled in the chair, we'll do a final walk-through of the service we agreed on and any adjustments that came out of the consultation. For locs, expect a relaxed pace — retwists run about ninety minutes to two hours depending on your head count and the method we're using. Starter locs and first-time installs run longer.

For braids, plan for a long session. Four to eight hours is normal for a full head of knotless, and longer for elaborate tribal feed-ins. I'll give you breaks, water, and a chance to stretch. Please eat before you come, and bring a snack for the middle if you know you get hungry.

For color, processing time happens with you comfortable in the chair, and I may run a quick second service during that window if scheduling allows. Color appointments generally run two to five hours.

Throughout, feel free to ask questions. I actually like when clients are curious — it's a sign you're going to take care of the hair between visits. Bring headphones, a laptop, a book, whatever makes you comfortable. This is your chair for the duration.

## Leaving the Chair: Aftercare Expectations

Before you leave, I'll walk you through aftercare for whatever we just did. For locs: when to wash, which products are fine, which to avoid, and when to come back for your next retwist. For braids: sleep on satin, mist your scalp, and how long this set should live before take-down. For color: don't wash for at least forty-eight hours, use color-safe products, and please don't swim in chlorine for the first week unless you've coated your hair in a heavy leave-in first.

You'll leave with my contact, so if anything feels off in the first few days you can text me. A little soreness on day one after braids is normal; pain, bumps, or sores are not, and I want to know about those fast so we can adjust.

Most clients book their next appointment before they leave, which helps me hold slots during busy seasons and helps you stay on a sustainable schedule.
